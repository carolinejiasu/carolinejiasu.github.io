Labor Economics

My independent research examines how high-skilled immigration policy can affect productivity, output, and regional economies.

AP Economics in tenth grade was my first real intro to economics, and I've been interested ever since. Coming from a family of immigrants, I'm aware that immigration policy encompasses far more than just data points. This drew me to labor economics.

Through my research, I sought to quantify the impact of high-skilled immigrants on our economy. I delved into a case study: Trump's 2017 Buy American, Hire American executive order. The policy placed much more scrutiny on H-1B immigrants, reducing visa approvals.

I found that although headcounts remained largely similar - likely due to concurrent tax cuts and deregulation - productivity declined in states more affected by the policy. I concluded that such restrictive policies often hold 'hidden' yet crucial costs.

My paper, “How High-Skill Immigration Restrictions Eroded Regional Productivity: Evidence from the 2017 BAHA Policy,” has been accepted for publication in the International Journal of High School Research.
